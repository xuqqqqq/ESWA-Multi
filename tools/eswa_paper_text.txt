Hybrid genetic algorithm                                   Selection (DCCVRPMRS). A Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Programming (MINP) model is constructed to minimize
                                                           the cumulative loss induced at all disaster nodes. We propose an improved Hybrid Genetic Algorithm (IHGA) to
                                                           solve the DCCVRPMRS. The algorithm includes four components: population initialization based on 𝑘-means and
                                                           random generation, route similarity-based crossover, a local search for delivery routes with an adaptive strategy,
                                                           and a local search for road selection purposes. Numerous experiments are conducted to compare the proposed
                                                           approach with other algorithms, validating the eﬀectiveness of IHGA. We also compare several delivery strategies
                                                           under diﬀerent objectives, priority factors, and disaster scenarios with cluster characteristics. The results indicate
                                                           that the clusters with the highest initial disaster degrees and deterioration rates are not always prioritized for
                                                           delivery. Moderate-to-high serious clusters that are concentrated and located near the depot generally have
                                                           higher delivery priority levels. Although prioritizing delivery to distant nodes with the most severe damage
                                                           reduces their losses, it signiﬁcantly increases the losses incurred in areas with concentrated clusters, increasing
                                                           the overall loss across all disaster nodes.

                                                                                     5.4. Analyses for priority to initial damage degree and deterioration rate

                                                                                         In a disaster, the ultimate objective of humanitarian relief is to min-
                                                                                     imize the loss for all disaster nodes, prioritizing nodes with severe dam-
                                                                                     age for supply deliveries. However, it is worth investigating whether
                                                                                     strictly following the principle of prioritizing the highest initial damage
                                                                                     or deterioration rate eﬀectively minimizes the overall loss. In this sub-
                                                                                     section, we employ two heuristic delivery strategies to optimize routes:
                                                                                     prioritizing the highest initial damage degree and prioritizing the high-
                                                                                     est deterioration rate. The deterioration rate of a disaster node is related
                                                                                     to both its natural deterioration rate and its recovery rate, and the de-
                                                                                                                        𝛽
                                                                                     terioration rate is calculated by 𝛾𝑖 for node 𝑖. The results are compared
                                                                                                                         𝑖
                                                                                     with those of the DCCVRPMRS and presented in Table 5. The results
                                                                                     indicate that the DCCVRPMRS, which comprehensively considers the
                                                                                     initial damage and deterioration rates, is more eﬀective at reducing the
                                                                                     cumulative loss. The strategy prioritizing the highest deterioration rate
                                                                                     also yields acceptable results, with a “Gap” of only approximately 1%,
                                                                                     outperforming the strategy focused on the highest initial damage. Given
                                                                                     the dynamic characteristics of the damage suﬀered by each node, high
                                                                                     deterioration rates pose a greater threat to disaster nodes. Taking the
                                                                                     initial damage as the most important criterion for optimizing the deliv-
                                                                                     ery sequence is not desirable. Although the strategy of prioritizing the
                                                                                     highest deterioration rate has advantages, comprehensively considering
                                                                                     both factors remains the best solution.
